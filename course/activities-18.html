    1. Recall the definition of a regular stochastic matrix.
    🔗
    \(\)
    🔗
    \({\mathbf{Definition}}\text{:}\)
    🔗
    A stochastic matrix \(P\) is \(\textbf{regular}\) if there is some \(k\) such that every entry of \(P^k\) is \(\framebox{________}.\)
    🔗
    \(\)
    🔗
    \(\)
    🔗
    Determine whether \(P\) and \(Q\) are a regular stochastic matrices.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} P = \begin{pmatrix} .8 \amp 0 \\ .2 \amp 1 \end{pmatrix}, \textup{ }Q = \frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 2 \\ 3 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    2. We use the idea of a regular stochastic matrix to describe convergence of Markov chains.
    🔗
    \({\mathbf{Theorem}}\text{:}\)
    🔗
    If \(P\) is a regular stochastic matrix, then \(\vec{x}_{k+1} = P\vec{x}_k\) converges to a \(\framebox{____________________}\) steady-state vector \(\vec{q}\) as \(k \rightarrow \infty\text{.}\)

    \({\large \textbf{15.2 Eigenvalue Problems}}\)
    1. The Markov Chains we were exploring have a connection to the eigenvalue problem.
    🔗
    \(\)
    🔗
    \({\mathbf{Definition}}\text{:}\)
    🔗
    If \(A\) is real \(n \times n\text{,}\) and there is a \(\vec{v} \neq \vec{0}\) in \(\mathbb{C}^n\text{,}\) and
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} A\vec{v} = \framebox{___________} \end{equation*}
    then \(\vec{v}\) is an \(\textbf{eigenvector}\) for \(A\text{,}\) and \(\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\) is the corresponding \(\textbf{eigenvalue}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \(\)
    🔗
    \(\)
    🔗
    (a) Is \(\lambda = 2\) an eigenvalue of \(A\text{?}\) Do not compute the characteristic polynomial.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \amp 2 \\ 3 \amp 8 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    \(\)
    🔗
    \(\)
    🔗
    \(\)
    🔗
    \(\)
    🔗
    (b) Determine whether \(\vec{v}_1\) and \(\vec{v}_2\) are eigenvectors of \(A\text{.}\) If so, what are their eigenvalues? Do not use the characteristic polynomial.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} A = \begin{pmatrix} -3 \amp -3 \amp 2 \\ 6 \amp 4 \amp 0 \\ 5 \amp 3 \amp 0 \end{pmatrix}, \textup{ } \vec{v}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \textup{ } \vec{v}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    \(\)
